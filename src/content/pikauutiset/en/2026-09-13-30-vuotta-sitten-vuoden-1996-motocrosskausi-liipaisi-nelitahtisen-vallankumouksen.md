The year 1996 is remembered for one of the most ferocious championship battles in the sport's history, the dominance of the Americans, and a technological breakthrough that permanently changed the future of motocross.

- **Emig dethroned the "King of Supercross":** After Jeremy McGrath's near-perfect supercross season (14 wins out of 15 races), Jeff Emig broke the winning streak outdoors and dramatically secured the AMA 250cc class championship at the season finale in Steel City.
- **New rulers of the World Championship:** Belgium's Stefan Everts captured his third world championship (250cc), 18-year-old French prodigy Sébastien Tortelli won the 125cc class, and New Zealand's Shayne King made history by winning the 500cc premier class championship on a KTM.
- **Team USA returned to the top:** The American team consisting of Jeremy McGrath, Jeff Emig, and Steve Lamson dominated the Motocross des Nations team world championship held in Jerez, Spain, and brought the victory back to the United States.
- **The modern four-stroke era began:** Yamaha announced that it had developed a lightweight and high-revving YZM400F four-stroke prototype, which sealed the end of the decades-long supremacy of two-stroke bikes.
