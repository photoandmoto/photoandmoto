The year 1986 is remembered in motocross history for an exceptional rule change that permanently altered racing bikes, as well as for the most dominant team performance in the history of the sport.

- **Historical "Production Rule":** The production bike rule that came into effect in the US AMA series banned the factory team prototypes that cost millions and forced teams to compete with bikes based on consumer models, which directly improved the quality of bikes available for purchase.
- **Team USA's dream ride in Maggiora:** The American trio of Rick Johnson, David Bailey, and Johnny O’Mara wiped the floor with other countries by taking 1-2 finishes in every moto at the Motocross des Nations. O’Mara was particularly memorable for passing the 500cc class leaders on his tiny 125cc Honda.
- **World Championship winners:** Great Britain's David Thorpe renewed his 500cc premier class championship on a Honda, Jacky Vimond made history as France's first world champion (250cc, Yamaha), and Dave Strijbos took the 125cc title on a Cagiva.
- **American rulers:** Rick Johnson, riding for Honda, was the top name in the USA, winning both the Supercross and the AMA 250cc outdoor championship. The heavier 500cc class title was taken by David Bailey and the 125cc class by Micky Dymond.
