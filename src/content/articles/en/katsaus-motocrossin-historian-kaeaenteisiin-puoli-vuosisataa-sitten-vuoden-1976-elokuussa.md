Belgian motocross legend Roger De Coster secured his fifth and final FIM 500cc motocross world championship in Ettelbrück, Luxembourg, on August 8, 1976. This FIM 500cc Grand Prix, which concluded the racing season, tested the riders' bodies beyond human limits during a time when the 1976 season culminated in a dramatic chain of events that defined the golden age of the sport.

### The Climax of the 500cc World Championship

Roger De Coster arrived at the final Grand Prix of the season in Luxembourg in the midst of a tight points battle against his Suzuki teammate Gerrit Wolsink. Wolsink, who worked as a dentist outside of racing, had won seven motos during the season, while De Coster had nine moto wins to his name. A torturous tension prevailed in the Suzuki pit tent, but in the end, De Coster managed to secure the world championship for himself with a razor-thin margin of just two points over his teammate. 

![Wolsink competed in the motocross world championship series from 1968 to 1982](/images/Wolsink.jpg "Wolsink competed in the motocross world championship series from 1968 to 1982")

While the Suzuki protégés fought tightly for the championship, a historic moment was seen on the track. KTM rider Jaak Van Velthoven dominated both starts and achieved the first-ever 500cc Grand Prix victory for the Austrian brand.

![Van Velthoven never won a motocross world championship; he is a three-time Belgian champion.](/images/van%20velthoven.jpg "Van Velthoven never won a motocross world championship; he is a three-time Belgian champion.")

> Disorder among the crowd cast a dark shadow over the historic final race when a spectator hanging from a tree caused British star rider Graham Noyce to crash violently at high speed. Noyce was injured in the incident and was rushed to the hospital due to the back injuries he sustained.

### The 1976 World Champions were determined at the end of the August races.

The final world champions of the 1976 international racing season were determined as the August races came to an end:

- **500cc**: Roger De Coster (Belgium, Suzuki) cemented his status as "The Man" by achieving the final world championship of his career.

- **250cc**: A historic achievement was made by the "Flying Finn" Heikki Mikkola (Finland, Husqvarna), who won the world championship by a one-point margin over KTM rider Gennadi Moisejev. With this, Mikkola became the first rider in history to hold both 250cc and 500cc class championships.

- **125cc**: Belgian Suzuki rider Gaston Rahier dominated this newly established lightweight class and took his second consecutive world championship.

### Meanwhile in America, it was the summer of the "Hurricane"

At the same time across the Atlantic, AMA motocross experienced an unprecedented transformation in August 1976. An unknown new talent, Bob "The Hurricane" Hannah, completely revolutionized the concept of how to ride a motocross bike.

Hannah had already amazed the world earlier that summer at Hangtown by coming from behind to pass Honda star rider Marty Smith for the win. By August 1976, he was piloting his revolutionary, liquid-cooled factory Yamaha OW27 bike at the top of the American series.

> Hannah's wild and aggressive "feet off the pegs" riding style changed motocross permanently, taking it toward a modern and high-flying era of extreme sports.
