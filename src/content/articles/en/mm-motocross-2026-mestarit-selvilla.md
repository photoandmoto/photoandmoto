## 2026 Motocross World Championship – Champions Decided

### Herlings' sixth title – First for Farres and Triumph

The Motocross World Championship season turned out to be interesting and nuanced, even though Dutchman Jeffrey Herlings ultimately dominated the MXGP class overwhelmingly and secured his sixth career world championship. In the MX2 class, however, the championship was contested by two Triumph riders right up to the final race in Australia, where Spain's Guillem Farres sealed the title. It was the first championship for Farres, as well as for Triumph.

Before the start of the World Championship season, there was a lot of interesting speculation, especially in the MXGP class. The series' two top names, Jeffrey Herlings and Slovenia's Tim Gajser, switched bike brands. Herlings had ridden for KTM for his entire career up to that point, a good 15 years, and now he jumped onto a factory Honda. Herlings had already won five championships with KTM; would his career continue just as brilliantly in the Japanese giant's team?

Gajser, for his part, moved from the long-standing factory Honda team to the factory Yamaha team after winning five championships on a Honda. The question was: how would the five-time world champions adapt to their new bikes and teams? Would either of them take a sixth championship?

Belgium's young and extremely hungry Lucas Coenen continued in the factory KTM team and had already shown his danger in the 2025 season. And what would the 2025 world champion, France's Romain Febvre, do? Febvre, riding for the factory Kawasaki team captained by Antti Pyrhönen, is known as a consistent and reliable rider, and that consistency is one of the keywords in motocross championships.

When the season kicked off in March in Argentina, Herlings immediately dispelled any doubts by winning both heats. Herlings' teammate, France's Tom Vialle, who returned to the World Championship series with a bang from America, grabbed the same amount of points (50) as Herlings. Febvre and Gajser were also well within the top five.

As the season progressed, Lucas Coenen began picking up wins alongside Herlings, and the season looked like it would become a battle between them until Coenen was injured and his season was practically over, even though he tried to return in the Lommel race in August, only to injure himself again. Thus, Herlings, who had continued his dominance, was on his way to his sixth championship, which was confirmed in the third-to-last race in Turkey. Herlings' superiority is well illustrated by the fact that he won every heat in the last eight races of the season (16 heat wins!). Throughout the season, he took a total of 22 heat wins. However, the champion's tough series also included three zero-point heats.

Febvre continued his steady and reliable work until the end of the season and took the World Championship silver that was on offer, with which he was also satisfied, even though he lost to Herlings by 168 points.
Gajser was ultimately third in the series, although he had to miss the last two races – China and Australia – due to injury. Vialle was fourth in points and Italy's Andrea Adamo fifth.

In the MX2 class, Triumph had entered the World Championship series with a completely new bike for the 2023 season, and already in the following season, 2024, Denmark's Mikkel Haarup piloted the fresh British bike to fifth place in the final World Championship standings. That made many eyes widen. Last season (2025), South Africa's Camden McLellan piloted the Triumph to fifth place in the World Championship series, which was already becoming a familiar position for Triumph riders! McLellan's teammate, Spain's Guillem Farres, was eighth in the final standings. However, the new season brought a jackpot for Triumph.

From the very beginning of the season, it was clear that Farres, McLellan, and Lucas's twin brother Sacha Coenen (KTM) would fight for the championship. Of them, Farres was able to do the most consistent and strongest work, and so 23-year-old Farres was crowned the new MX2 world champion. When teammate McLellan took the World Championship silver, the jackpot for Triumph was complete. Quite an achievement for the team; a championship brand in three years.

Sacha Coenen took the World Championship bronze, even though at some point in the season it looked like the Belgian would certainly fight for the championship. However, the results fluctuated quite a bit. Liam Everts, the third-generation GP winner of the Belgian Everts motocross dynasty, was fourth in points, and the previous season's champion, Germany's Simon Längenfelder, was left in fifth.
