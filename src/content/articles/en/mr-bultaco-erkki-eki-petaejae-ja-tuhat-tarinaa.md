Quiet Hyvinkää (population 32,270) slumbers through a beautiful Sunday morning on May 28, 1967. Two young rascals, Erkki Petäjä and Ami Toivonen, are wondering near the Hyvinkää Sveitsi area what is going on near the landfill, as cars loaded with rather peculiarly shaped motorcycles are heading there. They figure they should check it out, so the boys tighten the upper chains on their bicycles and pedal off to take a look. It turns out that some kind of motorcycle competition is taking place at the former landfill, near the current Kulomäki ski slope. The riders climb a steep uphill, turning left and right, dodging rocks, without their feet touching the ground. It turns out to be a national trial championship round.

> "At first, we watched and wondered what on earth they were doing. I had never seen a trial before. The bikes were quite peculiarly shaped gadgets, small saddles and really low down, too. Then it struck me that especially the Bultaco brand bikes went up the slope and through difficult spots really nicely, must be a good bike," recalls Erkki "Eki" Petäjä a good 60 years later.

> "In that race organized by Uudenmaan Moottorimiehet, Mauri Wallin and Markku Toivonen, who later became very familiar, were also competing. Wallin was fifth in the race and Toivonen sixth," Petäjä continues his story.

### More exposure to Bultaconism

Erkki Petäjä's next closer contact with motorcycle racing took place in the summer of 1968, when a motocross world championship round was held at the Erkylä locks in Hyvinkää.

> "I had previously joined the volunteer fire brigade youth section and back then I was at the cross races doing firefighter duties, so to speak, not really as a spectator. Still, that was my first personal experience of motocross".

![Bultaco Pursang 250 MK 2 1969-1971](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20250%20MK%204%201969-71%202.png "Bultaco Pursang 250 MK 2 1969-1971")

In the winter of 1970, an ice racing event was held on the Hyvinkää central sports field, right in the middle of town, on an oval track. That race has stayed precisely and permanently in Petäjä's mind! Little did Petäjä suspect where it would eventually lead.

> "We were watching the race there and I noticed that the overwhelming winner of the race was Reino Santala, and his bike was a Bultaco – the exact same brand that had been really good back in 1967 at that trial race, only this time Santala was riding the Pursang cross model. I thought that this Bultaco must be a good brand since it wins in trials and on ice," laughs Petäjä.

### And so Bultaco had shot Cupid's arrow into Petäjä's mind and heart.

> "That's how it happened. I ordered my very first own trial Bultaco Sherpa from Brandt on January 10, 1972. The delivery was slightly delayed and I didn't get it until August, but good things were worth the wait," Petäjä reminisces.

A funny story is also connected to Petäjä's first ever Bultaco order.

> "Jack Grönroos was working at Brandt at the time and handled my order. While I was waiting for my Bultaco to arrive, Grönroos had already moved to a new company and was now selling Montesas. One day the phone rang and Grönroos asked if I had received my Bultaco yet. I said I hadn't, to which he replied: ask for your order money back and come buy a Montesa. I answered that I'm buying a guitar so I won't be buying a violin," laughs Eki.

### Finally, Petäjä's long-awaited own Bultaco arrived.

> "When I went to pick up my Bultaco from Brandt, I transported it by train to Hyvinkää and pushed it home. At first, I didn't even have a place to store it, but Matti Tarkkonen said there was space in their garage, so my Bultaco was staying there for the first few nights."

![Bultaco Sherpa T250 Erkki Petäjän ensimmäinen pyörä](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T250%201970%20Erkki%20Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4%202.png "Bultaco Sherpa T250 Erkki Petäjä ensimmäinen pyörä")

In the summer of 1973, Petäjä participated in his first trial competition, which was held in Keimola. The most prominent memory from the race is traveling to the venue.

> "I didn't have any car at that time to transport the bike to the races. So the only option was to put gas in the tank, a backpack on my back, and ride to the races."

### The parts hunters

When it is a motorcycle brand that hasn't been manufactured since 1983, hunting for spare parts is an art form of its own. Erkki Petäjä, Mr. Bultaco, knows how to do that job.

> "You really have to do some pretty serious detective work sometimes to find the parts. So far, all parts have been found, one way or another. Once I happened to be at Brandt picking up some parts when it turned out they were selling off all their Bultaco spare parts, and once we quickly agreed on a price, I bought the whole lot so they wouldn't go to waste."

![Bultaco Pursang 125 MK5 1972](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20125%20MK5%201972%202.png "Bultaco Pursang 125 MK5 1972")

> "Sometime in the early 90s, a friend and I were traveling in Germany and had received information that former crosser Marcel Wiertz in Belgium had some parts available. So we pointed the car's nose toward Belgium, near the German border. Finally, after a lot of searching, we found [Marcel Wiertz](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Wiertz)'s motorcycle shop in a small village.

![Bultaco Pursang 250 MK9 1976](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20250%20MK9%201976%202.png "Bultaco Pursang 250 MK9 1976")

> "At first, we couldn't find a common language at all, but when I managed to tell him that I was from Finland and from Hyvinkää, Marcel sprang into action. He started repeating words in Finnish: Heikki Mikkola, motocross, Sveitsin maja, keskikalja, Erkylän lukot! And sure enough, parts started to be found," chuckles Petäjä. We could have easily spent a very long time as guests of Wiertz.

### Petäjä has a similar story from Spain.

> "In 2005, my wife and I were at a motorcycle market in Spain. I had models of Bultaco rear wheel spokes in my backpack, which I had been hunting for for a long time without success. And they weren't found this time either. The following year (2005), I struck gold. I met a guy there who was supposed to have all kinds of parts, including spokes. At first, the trading was a bit stiff, but when it turned out that I'm from Finland, the guy said he was a fan of Kimi Räikkönen and asked if I live far from Räikkönen's home?"

> "I answered that it's just over 50 kilometers away. So practically neighbors, the guy remarked, and the trading started to run smoothly. He makes any kind of spokes you want, so I ordered spokes from him and that deal went smoothly too. And the cooperation continues to this day."

![Bultaco Sherpa T125 1972](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T125%201972%202.png "Bultaco Sherpa T125 1972")

> "On that same trip, we were eating at a restaurant with my wife and Pepe Luhtasuo, and Pepe tipped us off that in the same restaurant was [Oriol Puig i Bultó](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriol_Puig_i_Bult%C3%B3), a man from the Bultaco family whose uncle was one of the owners of Bultaco. I went and asked him for an autograph on a T-shirt I had just bought. Not many people probably have one like that," reflects Petäjä.

![Oriol Buig y Bulton signeeraama paita](/images/Bultaco%20forver%202.png "Oriol Buig y Bulton signeeraama paita")

### Along the way, the collection has expanded

Today, Petäjä has 15 Bultacos in his garage, of which nine are Pursangs and six are Sherpas. Some people collect stamps, ballpoint pens, or beer cans; Petäjä just happens to collect Bultacos.

> "I don't really collect them as such, they've just accumulated. The latest arrival, which popped into the garage just in October, is a Bultaco Pursang MK9, a 250cc cross bike, 1976 model."

Petäjä's Bultaco fleet is the largest in Northern Europe. In France, two brothers have a staggering 40 Bultacos, which is probably one of the largest collections in the world.

Among Petäjä's Bultacos, there are also true rarities, such as a Bultaco Sherpa 350cc featuring the factory engine of three-time trial world champion (1976–1978) Yrjö Vesterinen. Even Ykä Vesterinen himself admired that engine when he comes from England to spend his holidays in Tammisaari during the summers, and I'm in contact with him from time to time.

![Bultaco Sherpa T350 runko 1978 moottori 1974](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T350%20runko%201978%20moottori%201974%202.png "Bultaco Sherpa T350 runko 1978 moottori 1974")

> "That engine also came to the garage as a result of a multi-stage chain of events. You could definitely make a whole book out of these bikes and parts hunting," chuckles the always cheerful Petäjä.

Whatever you want to know about Bultacos or whatever parts you might be missing, ask Eki. Mr. Bultaco is an encyclopedia where you are guaranteed to find the answer.
