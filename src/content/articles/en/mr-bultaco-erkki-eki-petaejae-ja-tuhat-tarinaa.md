Quiet Hyvinkää (32,270 inhabitants) is dozing through a beautiful Sunday morning on May 28, 1967. Two young lads, Erkki Petäjä and Ami Toivonen, are wondering near the Sveitsi area of Hyvinkää what is happening around the local landfill, as cars loaded with some rather peculiar-looking motorcycles are heading there. They decide they need to find out, so they tighten their bicycle chains and pedal off to take a look. It turns out that at the then-landfill, near the current Kulomäki ski slope, some kind of motorcycle competition is taking place. The riders climb a steep hill, turning left and right, dodging rocks, without their feet touching the ground. It becomes clear that it is a trial SM (Finnish Championship) sub-race.

> "At first, we were just looking, wondering what on earth they were doing. I had never seen a trial before. The bikes were also pretty strange-looking gadgets, small saddles and those too very low. Then I noticed that especially the Bultaco-branded bikes went up the slope and through the bad spots really nicely; it must be a good bike," recalls Erkki "Eki" Petäjä over 60 years later.

> "In that race organized by Uudenmaan Moottorimiehet, Mauri Wallin and Markku Toivonen, who later became very familiar names, were also riding. Wallin was fifth in the race and Toivonen was sixth," Petäjä continues his story.

### More exposure to Bultacoism

Erkki Petäjä's next closer contact with motorcycle racing took place in the summer of 1968, when a motocross World Championship sub-race was held at the Erkylä locks in Hyvinkää.

> "I had previously joined the volunteer fire department's youth section and was working at the cross race in a so-called firefighter capacity, not so much as a spectator. However, it was my first personal experience with motocross."

![Bultaco Pursang 250 MK 2 1969-1971](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20250%20MK%204%201969-71.png "Bultaco Pursang 250 MK 2 1969-1971")

In the winter of 1970, an ice racing competition was held at the Hyvinkää central field, right in the middle of the city, on an oval track. That race has remained in Petäjä's memory clearly and permanently! Little did Petäjä suspect where it would lead.

> "We were watching the race and I noticed that the superior rider of the race was Reino Santala and his bike was a Bultaco, the same brand as the ones that were really good back in 1967 at that trial race, only now Santala was riding the cross model, the Pursang. I thought that Bultaco must be a good brand if it wins in trials and on ice," laughs Petäjä.

### Thus, Bultaco had shot Cupid's arrow into Petäjä's mind and heart.

> "That's how it happened. I ordered my first own trial Bultaco (Sherpa) from Brandt on January 10, 1972. The delivery was slightly delayed and it only arrived in August, but it was worth the wait," Petäjä reminisces.

There is also a funny story associated with that first Bultaco order of Petäjä's.

> "Jack Grönroos was working at Brandt at the time and handled my order. While I was waiting for my Bultaco to arrive, Grönroos had already moved to a new company and was now selling Montesa. One day the phone rang and Grönroos asked if I had already received the Bultaco. I said I hadn't, to which he said, ask for your deposit back and come buy a Montesa. I replied that I am buying a guitar, so I won't buy a violin," laughs Eki.

### Finally, Petäjä's own long-awaited Bultaco arrived.

> "When I went to pick up my Bultaco from Brandt, I transported it by train to Hyvinkää and pushed it home. At first, I didn't even have a place to store it, but Matti Tarkkonen said there was room in their garage, so my Bultaco was there for the first few nights."

![Bultaco Sherpa T250 Erkki Petäjä's first bike](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T250%201970%20Erkki%20Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4.png "Bultaco Sherpa T250 Erkki Petäjä first bike")

In the summer of 1973, Petäjä participated in his first trial competition, which was held in Keimola. The most memorable thing about the race is how he got to the venue.

> "I didn't have a car at that time to transport the bike to the races. So the only option was to put gas in the tank, a backpack on my back, and ride to the races."

### The parts hunters

When it comes to a bike brand that hasn't been manufactured since 1983, hunting for spare parts is an art form of its own. That is a job Erkki Petäjä, Mr. Bultaco, knows well.

> "You really have to do some detective work sometimes to find the parts. So far, all parts have been found, one way or another. Once I happened to be at Brandt picking up some parts when it turned out they were selling off all their Bultaco spare parts, and once we quickly reached an agreement on the price, I bought everything off their hands."

![Bultaco Pursang 125 MK5 1972](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20125%20MK5%201972.png "Bultaco Pursang 125 MK5 1972")

> "Sometime in the early 90s, a friend and I were in Germany and had received information that some parts could be found in Belgium from ex-cross rider Marcel Wiertz. So we headed towards Belgium, near the German border. Finally, after a hard search, we found Marcel Wiertz's motorcycle shop in a small village.

![Bultaco Pursang 250 MK9 1976](/images/Bultaco%20Pursang%20250%20MK9%201976.png "Bultaco Pursang 250 MK9 1976")

> "At first, we couldn't find a common language at all, but when I managed to explain that I was from Finland and Hyvinkää, Marcel got excited. He repeated words in Finnish: Heikki Mikkola, motocross, Sveitsin maja, keskikalja, Erkylän lukot! And suddenly, parts started to appear," smiles Petäjä. We could have spent a long time as Wiertz's guests.

### Petäjä has a similar story from Spain.

> "In 2005, my wife and I were at a motorcycle market in Spain. I had samples of Bultaco rear wheel spokes in my backpack, which I had been hunting for a long time without success. And they weren't found then either. The following year (2005), I hit the jackpot. I met a guy there who was supposed to have all kinds of parts, including spokes. At first, the trading was a bit sluggish, but when it turned out I was from Finland, the guy said he was a Kimi Räikkönen fan and asked if I lived far from Räikkönen's home?"

> "I replied that it was just over 50 kilometers away. So almost neighbors, the guy thought, and the trading started to go smoothly. He makes any kind of spokes, so I ordered spokes from him, and that job was also handled smoothly. And the cooperation continues to this day."

![Bultaco Sherpa T125 1972](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T125%201972.png "Bultaco Sherpa T125 1972")

> "On the same trip, we were eating at a restaurant with my wife and Pepe Luhtasuo, and Pepe tipped me off that Oriol Buig y Bulto, a man from the Bultaco family whose uncle was one of the owners of Bultaco, was in the same restaurant. I went to ask him for an autograph on the T-shirt I had just bought. I don't think many people have one like that," reflects Petäjä.

![Shirt signed by Oriol Buig y Bulto](/images/Bultaco%20forver.png "Shirt signed by Oriol Buig y Bulto")

### The collection has expanded along the way

Nowadays, Petäjä has 15 Bultacos in his garage, nine of which are Pursangs and six are Sherpas. Some people collect stamps, ballpoint pens, or beer cans; Petäjä just happens to have Bultacos.

> "I don't really collect them, but they have just accumulated. The latest arrival, which popped into the garage just in October, is a Bultaco Pursang MK9, a 250cc cross bike, 1976 model."

Petäjä's Bultaco cavalcade is the largest in Northern Europe. In France, two brothers have a staggering 40 Bultacos, and that is likely one of the largest collections in the world.

Petäjä's group of Bultacos also includes real rarities, such as a Bultaco Sherpa 350cc, which has the engine of the factory bike of three-time (1976-1978) trial world champion Yrjö Vesterinen. Even Ykä Vesterinen himself marveled at that engine when he comes from England to spend his holidays in Tammisaari in the summers, and we are in contact from time to time.

![Bultaco Sherpa T350 frame 1978 engine 1974](/images/Bultaco%20Sherpa%20T350%20runko%201978%20moottori%201974.png "Bultaco Sherpa T350 frame 1978 engine 1974")

> "That engine also came to the garage as a result of a multi-stage chain of events. You could really write a book about these bikes and the hunting for parts," laughs the always good-humored Petäjä.

Whatever you want to know about Bultacos or whatever parts you are looking for, ask Eki. Mr. Bultaco is an encyclopedia that is guaranteed to have the answer.
